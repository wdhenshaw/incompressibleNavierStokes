\section{Introduction, governing equations, boundary conditions} \label{sec:introduction}





The \texttt{ins.m} Matlab code solves the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in velocity-pressure form,
\bse
\bat
  & \p_t \uv + (\uv\cdot\grad) \uv + \f{1}{\rho} \grad p = \nu \Delta \uv,   \quad && \xv\in\Omega, ~t>0,   \\
  &  \f{1}{\rho} \Delta p = - \grad\uv:\grad\uv,                                          \quad && \xv\in\Omega, ~t>0,  \\
  & \uv(\xv,0) = \uv_0(\xv)                                                  \quad && \xv\in\Omega,\\
  & \grad\cdot\uv(\xv,0) = 0                                                \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,
\eat
\ese
with appropriate boundary conditions as discussed below.


% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Boundary conditions}

Let $\nv(\xv)$ denote the outward unit normal vector to a point $\xv$ on a boundary.
Let $\tv$ denote the corresponding unit tangent vector.
The stress tensor is $\sigmav = -p \Iv + \tauv$ with $\tauv=\mu( \grad\uv + (\grad\uv)^T )$. They have components
\bse
\ba
   & \sigma_{ij} = - p \delta_{ij} + \mu ( \p_{x_j} u_i + \p_{x_i} u_j ), \\
   & \tau_{ij} =  \mu ( \p_{x_j} u_i + \p_{x_i} u_j ) . 
\ea
\ese
In two dimensions,
\ba
   \sigmav = \begin{bmatrix}
                 -p + 2 \mu u_x  & \mu( u_y + v_x ) \\
                  \mu( u_y + v_x ) &  -p + 2 \mu v_y
            \end{bmatrix}
\ea

\mni
Here are the physical boundary conditions. Numerically, additional compatibility conditions will be used.

\paragraph{\textbf{No-slip wall or inflow}}
\bse
\bat
   & \uv(\xv,t) = \gv(\xv,t),      \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0, \\
   & \grad\cdot\uv(\xv,t) = 0,     \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0.
\eat
\ese


\paragraph{\textbf{Slip wall}}
\bse
\bat
   & \nv^T\uv(\xv,t) = 0          \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0, \label{eq:slipWall1} \\
   & \tv^T\sigmav\nv = 0 ,        \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0, \label{eq:slipWall2} \\
   & \grad\cdot\uv(\xv,t) = 0     \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0.  \label{eq:slipWall3}
\eat
\ese
With explicit time-stepping, the slipwall condition~\eqref{eq:slipWall1} can be imposed by first updating the values on the boundary
using the interior PDE and then using~\eqref{eq:slipWall1} to set the normal component to zero,
\ba
    \uv \leftarrow \uv - (\nv^T\uv) \nv .
\ea
With implicit time-stepping, the slipwall condition~\eqref{eq:slipWall1} can be combined with the interior PDE to give the two equations
(thought of as determining the boundary points)
\ba
   \nv \nv^T \uv(\xv,t)  + (I-\nv\nv^T) ( \textrm{PDE} ) = 0 .
\ea
The slipWall conditions~\eqref{eq:slipWall2} and~\eqref{eq:slipWall2} can be written as (see comments below for traction BC)
\ba
   (\grad\cdot\uv(\xv,t)) \nv + (I-\nv\nv^T) \sigmav\nv = 0.
\ea
which re thought of as determining the ghost points.


\paragraph{\textbf{Outflow}}
\bse
\bat
   & a_0 p(\xv,t) + a_1 \p_n p(\xv,t) = g(\xv,t),    \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0, \\
   & \p_n^2(\tv\cdot\uv)                             \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0, \\
   & \grad\cdot\uv(\xv,t) = 0                        \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0.
\eat
\ese

\paragraph{\textbf{Pressure inflow}}
\bse
\bat
   & p(\xv,t) = g_1(\xv,t),            \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0, \\
   & \tv\cdot\uv = g_2                 \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0, \\
   & \grad\cdot\uv(\xv,t) = 0          \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0 .
\eat
\ese

\paragraph{\textbf{Traction (or free surface)}}
The traction (or free surface) boundary condition is 
\bse
\bat
   & \sigmav\nv = \gamma \kappa(\xv) \nv  ,             \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0,   \label{eq:tractionBC}\\
   & \grad\cdot\uv(\xv,t) = 0     \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega, ~t>0.
\eat
where $\gamma$ is the coefficient of surface tension and $\kappa$ is the curvature.
For a surface in 3D parameterized as $\yv(r,s)$, the curvature is 
\ba
   \kappa = \f{ \Big(\| \yv_r\|^2 \yv_{ss} -2 (\yv_r\cdot\yv_s)\yv_{rs} + \| \yv_s\|^2 \yv_{rr}\Big)\cdot\nv }{ \| \yv_r\|^2 \| \yv_s\|^2 -  (\yv_r\cdot\yv_s)^2 }.
\ea
For a curve $\yv(r)$ in 2D this reduces to
\ba
   \kappa = \f{ \yv_{rr} \cdot\nv }{ \| \yv_r\|^2  }.
\ea
The boundary condition $\sigmav\nv =  \gamma \kappa(\xv) \nv $ consist of the scalar equations $\nv^T\sigmav\nv=\gamma \kappa(\xv)$ and $\tv^T\sigmav\nv=0$ which are (with summation convection)
\bat  
   & -p + n_i \mu ( \p_{x_j} u_i + \p_{x_i} u_j ) n_j  = \gamma \kappa(\xv),       \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0, \\
   &  \mu \, t_i  ( \p_{x_j} u_i + \p_{x_i} u_j ) n_j  = 0,                        \quad && \xv\in\p\Omega,  ~t>0.
\eat
\ese

The boundary condition for the pressure on a traction boundary comes from the normal component of~\eqref{eq:tractionBC},
\ba
  \nv^T \sigmav \nv = \gamma \kappa(\xv).
\ea
In two dimensions
\ba
   \nv^T \sigmav \nv = - p + 2\mu\Big( u_x n_1^2 + v_y n_2^2 + (u_y + v_x)n_1 n_2 \Big).
\ea


The boundary conditions used for the velocity on a traction boundary are $\grad\cdot\uv=0$ and $\tv^T\tauv\nv=0$. These can be combined as
\ba
     (\grad\cdot\uv) \nv + \tv (\tv^T \tauv\nv)=0. \label{eq:tractionVelocityBCI}
\ea
From $I= \nv\nv^T + \tv\tv^T$, it follows that $\tv\tv^T = I - \nv\nv^T$ and thus~\eqref{eq:tractionVelocityBCI} can be written as 
\ba
     (\grad\cdot\uv) \nv + (I-\nv\nv^T) \tauv\nv=0 . \label{eq:tractionVelocityBC}
\ea
The form in~\eqref{eq:tractionVelocityBC} is convenient since it does not require forming tangent vectors. In addition, it is a good form to use when constucting 
the implicit time-stepping matrix to ensure that the matrix has non-zero diagonal entries.
